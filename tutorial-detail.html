How to Make a Wire Wrapped Labradorite Ring (12-Step Tutorial)

Wire wrapping is one of the most rewarding silversmithing techniques because it requires zero torch soldering! In this detailed guide, you will learn how to build a comfortable band and secure an oval labradorite gemstone with intricate sterling silver wraps.

Why Wire Wrapping is the Perfect Jewelry Skill

Whether you want to launch an Etsy jewelry shop, sell on artisan craft fairs, or create a viral Pinterest blog, wire wrapping is the gold standard gateway skill. Unlike traditional metalsmithing which requires a soldering torch, pickle pot, and flux, wire wrapping uses the tensile strength and malleability of precious metals to mechanically lock gemstones in place.

1

Preparing and Aligning the Base Frame Wires

Measure and cut 3 pieces of 20-gauge round sterling silver wire, each measuring approximately 10 inches (25 cm) long. Use your nylon jaw pliers to run over each wire to straighten any kinks and work-harden the metal slightly.

Align the three wires parallel to one another on your bench block. Secure them together temporarily with a piece of painter's tape on either end so they stay flush while you begin the weave.

Pro Jeweler Tip Always ensure you use Dead Soft wire. Half-hard wire is too stiff and will snap when performing tight weaves around the ring base.
2

Weaving the Ring Band (The 2-1 Basket Weave)

Take your 26-gauge weaving wire. Leave a 1-inch tail and begin wrapping around the bottom base wire 3 times to anchor it. Then wrap around the middle wire twice, and the top wire twice, before stepping back down.

Continue this consistent pattern for approximately 2.5 to 3 inches depending on your desired ring size (use our ring size chart: a US size 7 requires about 2.25 inches of woven band). Use your flat nose pliers to gently compress the weave together every few rounds so there are no visible gaps.

Tension Tip Keep your thumb firmly on the newly formed coils as you wrap to prevent the thin 26g wire from bunching or overlapping.
3

Sizing Around the Mandrel & Creating the Bezel Seat

Wrap the woven section around your steel ring mandrel at your desired size (e.g. Size 7). Press firmly with your fingers so the band takes a crisp, round curve.

Separate the unwoven 20g wire ends at both sides of the band. Bend the bottom two wires perpendicular to form a cradle/seat that your flat-backed Labradorite cabochon will rest against.

4

Securing the Labradorite & Wire Bezel Framing

Place your Labradorite cabochon securely onto the woven seat. Bring the outer 20g wires up along the girdle (perimeter) of the stone.

Using your round nose pliers, gently push the front wire over the bevel edge of the stone at a 45-degree angle. This creates the mechanical lock that holds the stone down firmly. Check from the side: the cabochon should not wiggle or shift.

Labradorite Protection Labradorite has a Mohs hardness of 6.0 to 6.5 with natural cleavage planes. Avoid pressing steel pliers directly into the face of the stone to prevent scratching its flash luster.
5

Decorative Filigree Sweeps & Anchoring

You will now have extra wire ends remaining. Instead of cutting them off, curve them into elegant organic sweeps and spirals around the sides of the setting (inspired by silver flower petals!).

Anchor the very tips of each wire by wrapping them twice around the main ring band, then cut with flush cutters on the *outside* of the ring. Use your chain nose pliers to press the cut tip flat against the band so it will never snag on clothing.

6

Polishing, Antiquing (Optional) & Final Reveal

Your ring is complete! For an antique artisan aesthetic, you can dip the ring in Liver of Sulfur (LOS) to oxidize the recesses of your weave, then buff the high points with a polishing cloth.

Wipe down the entire ring with a soft microfiber jewelry cloth to reveal the breathtaking blue-green flashes of the Labradorite set against gleaming sterling silver.
