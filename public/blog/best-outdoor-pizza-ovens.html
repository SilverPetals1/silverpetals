What to Look for When Buying an Outdoor Pizza Oven

Buying an outdoor pizza oven is about more than choosing the model with the highest advertised temperature. The best option depends on your cooking habits, available space, budget, and preferred fuel.

Here are the most important factors to consider before purchasing.

1. Fuel Type

Outdoor pizza ovens generally use gas, wood, charcoal, or electricity.

Gas pizza ovens: These are convenient for everyday use. They heat up without requiring you to manage a wood fire, and flame controls make temperature adjustments easier.

Wood-fired ovens: These are ideal for people who enjoy traditional live-fire cooking. They require more attention but offer a distinctive cooking experience.

Multi-fuel ovens: These provide more flexibility by supporting different fuel options. They are useful if you want to experiment with wood-fired cooking while keeping gas as an alternative.

Electric outdoor ovens: These can be convenient where a suitable electrical supply is available. Always check that the specific appliance is approved for outdoor use.

2. Cooking Capacity

Pizza oven sizes vary considerably. Compact models are generally designed for pizzas around 12 inches, while larger ovens can accommodate 16-inch or even bigger pizzas.

Think about how many people you usually cook for. A smaller oven may be enough for two or three people, while a larger cooking surface is more practical for parties and family gatherings.

Remember that the advertised maximum pizza size is not always the most comfortable size to handle. Leave enough room to launch, rotate, and remove the pizza safely.

3. Maximum Temperature

High temperatures are essential for making Neapolitan-style pizza with a light, airy crust and a properly cooked base.

Many dedicated pizza ovens are designed to reach temperatures around 800–950°F, although actual performance depends on fuel, weather, preheating, and cooking conditions.

A high maximum temperature is useful, but heat distribution and stone recovery between pizzas are equally important.

4. Portability and Storage

If you have a permanent outdoor kitchen, a larger oven may be perfectly suitable. However, people with smaller patios or limited storage should consider compact models.

For camping or travel, look at the oven's weight, dimensions, fuel setup, and the manufacturer's transportation guidance.

5. Build Quality and Insulation

A well-built oven should be designed to handle repeated exposure to high cooking temperatures. Insulation can also help maintain heat and improve cooking consistency.

Look for durable exterior materials, a suitable stone cooking surface, stable construction, and clear manufacturer instructions for cleaning and maintenance.

6. Accessories

Some pizza ovens include useful accessories, while others require separate purchases. Common accessories include:

  • Pizza peel: For launching and removing pizzas smoothly
  • Turning peel: For rotating pizzas inside the high-heat oven
  • Infrared thermometer: For checking stone temperature accurately
  • Protective weather cover: For durable outdoor storage
  • Suitable pizza rocker/cutter: For clean slice presentation
  • Heat-resistant gloves: For safety around 900°F heat

Tips for Making Better Pizza in an Outdoor Oven

Even an expensive pizza oven cannot guarantee excellent results without the right technique. A few simple habits can make a noticeable difference:

  • Preheat the oven properly: Give the oven enough time to heat according to the manufacturer's instructions. The cooking stone needs to become hot, not just the air inside the oven.
  • Check the stone temperature: An infrared thermometer can help you understand whether the cooking surface is ready before launching.
  • Avoid too much topping: Excessive sauce, cheese, and wet ingredients can make the center soggy.
  • Use a properly prepared pizza peel: A lightly floured or semolina-dusted peel can help the pizza slide into the oven more easily without sticking.
  • Rotate when needed: Many pizza ovens have stronger heat near the burner. Turning the pizza 90–180 degrees every 20–30 seconds helps the crust cook evenly.
  • Allow the stone to recover: If you're making several pizzas in succession, give the stone 2–3 minutes to regain heat between cooks.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the best outdoor pizza oven in 2027?

The Ooni Karu 2 Pro is a strong overall choice for buyers who want multi-fuel flexibility and a larger cooking area. The Gozney Arc XL is another excellent option for people who prefer a premium gas-powered oven.

2. Are outdoor pizza ovens worth buying?

Yes, if you enjoy homemade pizza and frequently cook outdoors. These ovens can reach much higher temperatures (800–950°F) than standard household ovens (500–550°F), helping create crisp bases and well-developed crusts in under 90 seconds.

3. Is gas or wood better for a pizza oven?

Gas is generally easier to operate and control with precise temperature dials. Wood is better suited to people who enjoy traditional live-fire cooking and don't mind managing the flame for subtle smoke nuance.

4. How hot should an outdoor pizza oven get?

Many dedicated pizza ovens are designed to reach approximately 800–950°F (430–500°C). The ideal temperature depends on the pizza style, dough recipe hydration, and cooking method.

5. Can you cook more than pizza in an outdoor pizza oven?

Yes. Depending on the model and manufacturer's instructions, outdoor pizza ovens can also be used for flatbreads, roasted vegetables, cast-iron seared steaks, roasted poultry, and other high-temperature culinary dishes.

6. Do outdoor pizza ovens need special accessories?

They don't always require accessories to operate, but a pizza peel is highly useful. A turning peel and infrared thermometer can also make cooking easier and much more consistent.

7. Can an outdoor pizza oven stay outside?

Some models are designed for outdoor use with weather-resistant coatings, but repeated exposure to rain, snow, and extreme weather can damage components over time. Follow the manufacturer's storage instructions and use a waterproof protective cover when left outside.

8. Are electric outdoor pizza ovens better than gas models?

Electric ovens offer convenient push-button temperature controls without propane tanks, while gas ovens are often easier to use in locations without an outdoor electrical outlet. The right choice depends on your patio setup and cooking preferences.
