How to Choose the Best Electric Smoker

Before purchasing an electric smoker, consider your cooking habits, available space, and preferred level of control.

1. Cooking Capacity

Cooking capacity determines how much food you can prepare at once. A smaller smoker may be enough for a couple or small family, while a larger cabinet can be more practical for gatherings and batch cooking. Don't compare only the total square inches advertised by manufacturers—check the dimensions of individual racks and the clearance between them.

2. Temperature Range

Most traditional electric cabinet smokers are designed primarily for low-and-slow cooking, with maximum temperatures around 250–275°F. This is suitable for brisket, pork shoulder, ribs, chicken, fish, and sausages. However, if you want high-temperature grilling or crisp poultry skin, a pellet grill or conventional grill may be a better choice.

3. Digital vs. Analog Controls

Electric smokers generally offer two types of temperature controls:

  • Digital controls: Allow you to select exact target temperatures and may include integrated meat probes, timers, and Wi-Fi features.
  • Analog controls: Use a simple rheostat dial. They have fewer electronics to maintain but require manual thermometer monitoring.

4. Wood-Chip Loading System

Some models use an internal wood-chip tray, while others include a side-loading system that lets you add chips without opening the main cabinet. Bradley smokers use automatically fed wood bisquettes. Think about how often you want to refill smoking fuel during long cooks.

5. Insulation and Construction

Insulation helps an electric smoker retain heat, which is essential in cooler or windy outdoor conditions. Stainless-steel double-walled cabinets offer superior thermal efficiency and long-term rust resistance.

6. Cleaning and Maintenance

Look for removable drip pans, accessible heating elements, easy-to-clean chrome or stainless racks, and practical grease-management systems. A smoker that is easy to wipe down ensures regular, hassle-free use.

7. Electrical Compatibility

Electric smokers require a suitable outdoor electrical supply. Verify required voltage (typically standard 120V in the US), wattage, and manufacturer-approved outdoor use guidelines. Always connect to a GFCI-protected outlet.

Electric Smoker vs. Pellet Smoker: Which Is Better?

Electric smokers and pellet smokers both use electricity, but they operate quite differently. An electric cabinet smoker uses an electric heating element to cook food while wood chips provide flavor. A pellet smoker burns compressed wood pellets in a motorized firepot to generate both heat and smoke.

Electric Smoker Safety Tips

  • Always use the smoker outdoors in a well-ventilated area—never operate it inside a home, garage, tent, or enclosed porch due to carbon monoxide and smoke hazards.
  • Connect only to a properly grounded, GFCI-protected outdoor outlet.
  • Keep electrical connections and cords dry and elevated off damp ground.
  • Avoid using lightweight household extension cords; use heavy-duty outdoor-rated cords if necessary.
  • Maintain at least 10 feet of clearance from combustible walls, wood railings, and overhangs.
  • Clean grease trays and catch pans regularly to prevent flare-ups.
  • Allow the unit to cool down completely before cleaning or covering with an outdoor weather cover.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are electric smokers good for beginners?

Yes. Electric smokers are exceptionally beginner-friendly because they eliminate the difficulty of fire management. You simply set your temperature, add wood chips, and let the heating element do the work.

What is the best electric smoker for most people?

The Masterbuilt 30-Inch Digital Electric Smoker is a great starting point for most households due to its side wood-chip loader, dependable digital controls, and affordable price point.

Can you smoke brisket in an electric smoker?

Yes, as long as your cabinet is wide enough. A full packer brisket fits comfortably in a 40-inch model like the Masterbuilt 40-inch, or you can separate the flat and point to fit on 30-inch racks.

Do electric smokers produce enough smoke flavor?

Electric smokers produce great smoke flavor, though slightly lighter than an all-wood offset stick burner. Using quality hardwood chunks and maintaining wood replenishment during the first 3–4 hours produces a rich, appetizing flavor profile.

Are electric smokers expensive to operate?

No. An electric smoker typically draws 800 to 1200 watts. Running one for 8 hours usually costs less than $1.00 in electricity, making it far cheaper to run than propane or bag-charcoal setups.

Can you use an electric smoker in a garage?

No. Electric smokers generate real wood smoke and carbon monoxide. Operating one in an enclosed garage is a serious fire hazard and health risk. Always use outdoors.
