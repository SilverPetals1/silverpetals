Best Wooden Cutting Board for Raw Meat: 7 Durable, Safe, and Easy-to-Clean Picks

H
Written by SilverPetals • September 22, 2026 • 12 min read

Preparing chicken, beef, pork, or seafood at home requires more than a sharp knife. Your cutting surface also matters.

A good wooden cutting board for raw meat should be large enough to work comfortably, stable while chopping, resistant to everyday knife marks, and—ideally—equipped with a juice groove that helps keep meat juices from running across the counter.

But food safety comes first.

According to the USDA, consumers can use either wood or nonporous cutting boards for raw meat and poultry. However, the USDA recommends keeping a separate board for raw meat, poultry, and seafood when possible so bacteria are not transferred to bread, vegetables, or other foods that may be eaten without cooking. Cutting boards should also be washed with hot, soapy water after use.

After comparing hardwood type, dimensions, thickness, juice management, stability, reversibility, and maintenance requirements, our overall pick is the John Boos Maple CB Series Cutting Board with Juice Groove.

However, it is not the right board for every kitchen.

Below are seven wooden boards worth considering, including maple, teak, acacia, and walnut options at different sizes and price levels.

1. John Boos Maple CB Series — Best Overall

If you want one substantial wooden board dedicated to raw meat preparation, the John Boos Maple CB Series is our strongest overall choice.

The 20-by-15-inch version uses Northern Hard Rock Maple and is 1.5 inches thick. It uses edge-grain construction and includes a juice groove on one side.

That combination makes a lot of sense when working with steaks, chicken pieces, roasts, or other ingredients that can release liquid during preparation.

The board is reversible, so you also have a flat working surface on the opposite side. Recessed finger grips make a heavy board easier to lift from the counter.

John Boos currently offers this model in both 20-by-15-inch and 24-by-18-inch versions. The smaller version weighs around 12 pounds, so it is substantial enough to stay relatively stable during cutting.

What makes this board stand out is the balance between working space and thickness.

A thin board can move around more easily, while a very large butcher block can become annoying to carry to the sink after handling raw meat. The 20-by-15-inch version offers a useful middle ground.

Maple is also a classic cutting-board hardwood and provides a much more knife-friendly surface than glass or stone.

This is not a low-maintenance dishwasher-style cutting board. Wood should be cleaned promptly after raw-meat preparation and allowed to dry thoroughly. Regular conditioning with food-grade board oil can also help prevent excessive drying.

The board is fairly heavy, which is excellent for stability but less convenient for anyone who needs to store it in a high cabinet after every use.

Home cooks who regularly prepare raw meat and want a substantial maple board with a useful juice groove.

2. Teakhaus Traditional Carving Board 109 — Best Teak Cutting Board

The Teakhaus Traditional Carving Board 109 is an excellent alternative if you prefer teak instead of maple.

It measures approximately 20 by 15 inches and is 1.5 inches thick. The board uses edge-grain teak and has a perimeter juice canal, reversible construction, and built-in hand grips.

Teakhaus states that this board is made from FSC-certified teak. The manufacturer recommends hand washing, towel drying, and applying food-grade mineral oil regularly.

One reason teak is popular for cutting boards is its natural oil content. Teakhaus specifically highlights moisture resistance as one of the advantages of its teak boards.

That does not mean you can leave raw-meat juices sitting on the board. You still need proper cleaning and drying after every use. But for someone who wants a robust wood board that handles regular kitchen prep, teak is an attractive choice.

This is probably the feature that matters most for this article. The perimeter groove can catch liquid before it reaches the countertop. That is helpful while trimming steaks, portioning poultry, or working with larger cuts. The opposite side is flat, which gives you another working surface.

At around 12 pounds, the medium board is not lightweight. You should make sure your sink and storage area can comfortably accommodate a 20-by-15-inch board.

Anyone wanting a durable teak cutting board with a generous juice canal and enough space for larger meat preparation.

3. Sonder Los Angeles Winsome — Best Value Acacia Board

You do not necessarily need a huge premium butcher block for everyday chicken breasts, steaks, or smaller cuts.

The Sonder Los Angeles Winsome Acacia Cutting Board measures approximately 16 by 12 inches and is 1.5 inches thick.

It uses edge-grain acacia, weighs around six pounds, and includes a juice groove, finger grips, and a reversible design. Sonder states that the groove can hold approximately 3.5 ounces of liquid. The opposite side includes the company's built-in Cracker Well.

Its smaller footprint is the main advantage. A 24-inch board is fantastic when breaking down large ingredients, but it can be overkill for a small apartment kitchen. The Winsome is easier to move while still providing a thick working surface. The integrated finger grips also make it easier to lift without sliding your fingers underneath a wet board.

Sonder recommends hand washing with mild dish soap rather than placing the board in a dishwasher or soaking it in water. The company also recommends periodic treatment with food-grade mineral oil or beeswax.

Smaller kitchens and shoppers who want a thick wooden board without buying an oversized butcher block.

4. John Boos Maple AuJus Board — Best for Controlling Meat Juices

If liquid management is one of your biggest concerns, look at the John Boos Maple AuJus Cutting Board.

Instead of a basic groove, this model uses a sloped juice groove designed to help collect liquid. The 20-by-15-inch model is 1.5 inches thick and made from Northern Hard Rock Maple. It is also reversible and includes recessed finger grips. John Boos lists the AuJus board as NSF certified.

Most wooden cutting boards have a narrow channel running around the outside edge. That is useful, but if you regularly prepare or carve foods that release more liquid, a more substantial juice-management design may be useful. The AuJus is therefore particularly attractive for meat-focused kitchens.

Remember that raw-meat preparation and carving cooked meat should ideally not happen on an unwashed surface. If you have used the board for raw chicken or beef, clean and sanitize it properly before using it again for cooked food. USDA warns against putting cooked food onto surfaces or utensils that previously held raw meat unless they have first been properly cleaned.

It is a premium product and may be unnecessary if you prepare meat only occasionally.

Serious home cooks, BBQ enthusiasts, and households that regularly work with larger or juicier cuts.

If aesthetics matter almost as much as performance, the Sonder Los Angeles Franklin Cutting Board deserves consideration.

It uses American Black Walnut and measures approximately 20 by 15 by 1.5 inches. The board has edge-grain construction, a juice groove, finger grips, and removable rubber feet. It weighs around 10.3 pounds.

A stable cutting surface matters whenever you are using a kitchen knife. The removable feet can help provide stability, while removing them gives you more flexibility in how the board is used.

Walnut tends to cost more than basic acacia options. And like other quality wooden boards, it requires manual cleaning and periodic maintenance.

Someone who wants a premium dark-wood board that can remain part of the kitchen decor instead of being hidden in a cabinet.

6. Teakhaus Essential 405 — Best Lightweight Choice

Heavy cutting boards are stable, but there is one obvious disadvantage: cleaning them can become a chore. The Teakhaus Essential 405 addresses that problem.

It measures around 16 by 11 inches, is approximately 0.55 inches thick, and weighs only about two pounds. Despite the slimmer construction, it still includes a perimeter juice canal. After handling raw meat, you want to clean your cutting board immediately. A smaller two-pound board is much easier to carry to the sink than a 12- or 18-pound butcher block. That convenience could matter more than extreme thickness for some households.

A lightweight board will not feel as planted on the counter as a thick block. Placing a clean damp kitchen towel or suitable non-slip mat underneath can help stabilize a board while you work. Do not sacrifice a stable cutting surface simply to save weight.

Small kitchens, occasional meat preparation, or users who struggle to lift heavy wooden boards.

What Makes a Good Wooden Cutting Board for Raw Meat?

The best board is not necessarily the most expensive one. For raw meat, there are several factors that matter more.

This is the most important recommendation in this entire guide. The USDA recommends, when possible, keeping one cutting board for raw meat, poultry, and seafood and another for bread, vegetables, fruit, and other foods that may not be cooked before eating. Even an expensive wood board can become a cross-contamination problem if it is handled incorrectly.

Raw meat can release liquid during trimming and portioning. A perimeter groove provides somewhere for that liquid to collect instead of allowing it to immediately reach your countertop. Think of it as spill management rather than a food-safety system.

For everyday meat preparation, around 16 by 12 inches can work well. For larger cuts or whole poultry, something closer to 20 by 15 inches provides significantly more working room. A 1.5-inch board generally feels more substantial and stable than an extremely thin board, though a thinner board is lighter to carry to the sink.

Maple is a classic choice with a light appearance and long history in professional-style butcher blocks. Teak contains natural oils and is valued for moisture resistance. Walnut provides a softer, darker premium appearance. Acacia can provide excellent value while still delivering a substantial hardwood surface.

After preparing raw meat, wash the board promptly with hot, soapy water. Rinse it with clean water and dry it rather than leaving it sitting wet.

USDA guidance also says wooden and plastic cutting boards can be sanitized using a solution made with 1 tablespoon of unscented liquid chlorine bleach per gallon of water. The surface should be flooded with the solution, allowed to stand for several minutes, then rinsed with clean water and air-dried or dried with clean paper towels.

Always consider the board manufacturer's care instructions as well. Many premium laminated wooden boards specifically advise against dishwasher use, soaking, or prolonged exposure to water. Once a wooden cutting board develops deep, hard-to-clean grooves or excessive wear, USDA recommends replacing it.

A wooden cutting board can absolutely be used for raw meat when handled properly. However, plastic has one major advantage: nonporous materials are easier to clean than wood, according to USDA guidance.

Wood has other advantages. It is generally gentle on quality knife edges, can last for years when maintained properly, and many users prefer its weight and feel while chopping. If you prefer the cutting feel, durability, appearance, and stability of hardwood, a dedicated wooden meat board can be an excellent choice—as long as you commit to cleaning it properly every time.

The best wooden cutting board for raw meat should make preparation easier without making safe cleanup unnecessarily difficult.

For most kitchens, our top choice is the John Boos Maple CB Series. Its 20-by-15-inch working area gives you plenty of preparation space, the 1.5-inch construction provides a substantial cutting surface, and the perimeter groove helps control meat juices.

For teak lovers, the Teakhaus Traditional 109 is an excellent alternative. If you have a smaller kitchen or want something more affordable, the Sonder Winsome Acacia Cutting Board provides a useful combination of size, thickness, and juice management. For maximum juice control, consider the John Boos AuJus. And if cleaning a heavy board is difficult for you, the lightweight Teakhaus Essential 405 may be the more practical choice.

Most importantly, whichever board you buy, dedicate it to raw meat when possible, wash it thoroughly after every use, and replace it when deep cuts become difficult to clean.
