6 Best Carpet Cleaners of 2027: Top Machines for Deep Cleaning

S
Written by SilverPetals Editorial Team • November 19, 2026 • 12 min read

Carpets make a home feel cozy and comfortable, but they also collect dirt, dust, spills, pet hair, and stubborn stains. Regular vacuuming helps remove loose debris, but it cannot always remove the grime and stains trapped deep inside carpet fibers.

That's where a dedicated carpet cleaner can help.

Unlike a regular vacuum, a carpet cleaning machine sprays water and cleaning solution into the carpet, agitates the fibers, and extracts dirty water. Some models are designed for cleaning entire rooms, while portable machines are better suited to furniture, stairs, car interiors, and small spills.

The best carpet cleaner for your home depends on how much carpet you have, whether you own pets, and how frequently you need to remove stains.

In this guide, we'll look at six carpet cleaners worth considering in 2027, from full-size deep-cleaning machines to convenient portable spot cleaners.

How to Choose the Best Carpet Cleaner

Buying a carpet cleaner involves more than comparing brands and prices. The right machine should match your carpet type, cleaning frequency, household size, and the kinds of stains you usually encounter.

Here are the most important factors to consider.

1. Choose the Right Machine Type

Carpet cleaners generally fall into three main categories:

Full-size upright carpet cleaners: These machines are designed to wash larger carpeted areas. They usually include separate water tanks, powered brushes, and wider cleaning paths. Best for wall-to-wall carpets, multiple rooms, regular deep cleaning, and larger households.

Portable spot cleaners: Portable machines use a hose and small cleaning attachment to treat specific areas. Best for small spills, pet accidents, upholstery, stairs, and vehicle interiors.

Professional-style carpet cleaners: These machines are often larger and designed for substantial cleaning jobs. They can have larger tanks and heavier construction. Best for large carpeted homes, heavily soiled carpets, and frequent deep cleaning.

2. Check the Brush System

The brush system helps loosen dirt and stains trapped in carpet fibers. Look for a powered brush or rotating brush system if you want to clean heavily used carpets. Some models have multiple brush rolls, while others use a single rotating brush. Always check the carpet manufacturer's care instructions before using a wet cleaner.

3. Compare Water Tank Capacity

Carpet cleaners generally have two tanks: a clean-water tank for fresh water and cleaning solution, and a dirty-water tank that collects extracted moisture and debris. Larger tanks reduce refilling trips, but add weight when filled. Balance tank size with your home's square footage.

4. Consider Drying Time

A carpet cleaner does not instantly dry the carpet after washing. It extracts dirty water, but some moisture remains in the fibers. Drying time depends on carpet thickness, cleaning passes, water extraction efficiency, room temperature, and air circulation. Choose a machine with effective extraction and avoid over-saturating carpet fibers.

5. Look for Pet-Stain Features

Pet owners often need to remove more than ordinary dirt. Urine, muddy paw prints, and unpleasant odors can require specialized cleaning solutions. Useful features include targeted stain attachments, pet-specific cleaning formulas, hose attachments, and easy-to-rinse dirty water tanks.

6. Check Cleaning Solution Compatibility

Not every cleaning formula is suitable for every machine. Before buying, check recommended cleaning solutions, concentration requirements, and fabric suitability. Using the wrong chemical can void warranties or leave detergent residue behind.

7. Consider Storage and Maintenance

Carpet cleaners need regular maintenance to keep working properly. After each cleaning session, empty and rinse both tanks, clean the brush rolls, and rinse the hose. In smaller homes or apartments, verify closet clearance before choosing an industrial upright model.

Carpet Cleaner vs. Carpet Vacuum: What's the Difference?

A carpet vacuum and a carpet cleaner perform fundamentally different jobs.

Feature Carpet Vacuum Carpet Cleaner
Main Purpose Removes dry surface dirt and loose debris Washes, shampoos, and extracts deep grime
Uses Water No (dry suction only) Yes (warm water injection)
Cleaning Solution No Yes (specialized shampoo formulas)
Removes Loose Dust Primary function Secondary (vacuum first recommended)
Treats Liquid Stains Very limited Specifically engineered for spills & stains
Drying Time None required Requires several hours to dry

A vacuum is useful for regular maintenance, while a carpet cleaner is designed for periodic washing and stain treatment. For optimal floor care, many households benefit from using both.

Common Carpet Cleaning Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using too much cleaning solution: Excess detergent leaves residue in carpet fibers that attracts dirt faster. Always follow manufacturer measuring caps.
  • Over-wetting the carpet: Excessive moisture extends drying times and can seep into subflooring. Keep passes balanced and extraction thorough.
  • Ignoring carpet manufacturer instructions: Certain wool or delicate natural fiber carpets require dry extraction rather than wet shampooing.
  • Skipping the dry vacuuming step: Always vacuum thoroughly first to remove grit, hair, and dust before introducing moisture.
  • Not cleaning the machine after use: Rinsing tanks and removing brush roll tangles immediately prevents odor and mildew buildup.
  • Walking on damp carpet too soon: Allow adequate drying time before foot traffic to preserve plush pile texture.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the best carpet cleaner for home use?

The Shark CarpetXpert EX151 is a strong option to consider for general home use. Buyers who want additional steam-assisted cleaning features may prefer the BISSELL Revolution HydroSteam, while the Hoover SmartWash+ is worth considering for its automatic cleaning operation.

2. Is a carpet cleaner better than a regular vacuum?

They perform different jobs. A vacuum removes dry dirt, dust, and loose debris, while a carpet cleaner washes carpet using water and cleaning solution and extracts dirty moisture.

3. How often should I deep-clean my carpet?

It depends on foot traffic, pets, children, and household conditions. Many homes benefit from periodic deep cleaning every 6–12 months, while high-traffic areas and homes with pets may need more frequent attention.

4. Can I use a carpet cleaner on upholstery?

Some carpet cleaners include hose attachments designed for upholstery. However, not every fabric is suitable for wet extraction. Check the furniture manufacturer's cleaning code and the carpet cleaner's instructions before using it.

5. Are portable carpet cleaners worth buying?

Yes, if you mainly need to remove small spills, pet stains, and upholstery messes. They are convenient and easy to store. However, they are generally not the best choice for washing large carpeted rooms.

6. How long does carpet take to dry after cleaning?

Drying time varies depending on carpet thickness, humidity, ventilation, and the machine's water extraction performance. Some carpets may dry within several hours, while thicker carpets can take longer.

7. Can carpet cleaners remove pet urine smells?

They can help remove surface contamination when used with an appropriate pet-stain cleaning formula. However, urine that has soaked into carpet padding may require additional treatment or professional cleaning.

8. Should I vacuum before using a carpet cleaner?

Yes. Vacuuming first removes loose dirt, dust, hair, and debris. This helps prepare the carpet for wet cleaning and protects the cleaner's internal suction motors.
