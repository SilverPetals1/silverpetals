How to Choose the Best Gas Grill Under $500

Before purchasing a gas grill, think about your cooking habits, available outdoor space, and the features you will actually use regularly.

1. Number of Burners

The number of burners affects how you can divide the cooking surface into heat zones. Two burners can be sufficient for smaller households. Three burners offer a useful balance of space and flexibility. Four burners are helpful when preparing multiple foods or larger meals. Keep in mind that more burners do not automatically mean better cooking performance—burner arrangement and heat distribution are equally important.

2. Cooking Area

Always compare the primary cooking area rather than relying only on the total advertised square inches, as some manufacturers include warming racks in their headline numbers. If you regularly cook for several people, look for enough primary grate space to prepare your usual meals without overcrowding the grill.

3. Cooking Grate Material

Cooking grates influence heat retention, maintenance, and durability:

  • Porcelain-enameled cast iron: Holds heat well and can produce attractive sear marks. It needs gentle handling to avoid chipping the coating.
  • Stainless steel: Generally easier to maintain and resistant to rust, depending on its grade and construction.
  • Porcelain-coated steel: Often found on more affordable grills, but coating wear over time can affect long-term performance.

4. BTU Output

BTU measures the total heat output of a grill's burners, but it should not be the only specification you consider. A higher BTU number does not automatically mean better cooking results. Look at burner layout, cooking-area size, lid design, heat distribution, and real-world performance when comparing models.

5. Side Burner and Searing Zone

A side burner can help prepare sauces and side dishes while the main grill is occupied. A dedicated searing zone can be useful for cooking steaks at higher temperatures. If you rarely use these features, a simpler grill may offer better value.

6. Cleaning and Grease Management

Look for removable grease trays, accessible catch pans, and cooking grates that are easy to brush down. Regular maintenance helps prevent dangerous grease buildup and supports safer operation.

7. Warranty and Replacement Parts

A gas grill is an outdoor appliance exposed to high heat, weather, and regular use. Before purchasing, check the warranty for burners, cooking grates, lid, and other components, and confirm that replacement parts are readily available.

Gas Grill Safety Tips

  • Always use the fuel type (liquid propane or natural gas) specified by the manufacturer.
  • Check gas connections and hoses for leaks using soapy water before the grilling season.
  • Always keep the grill outdoors in a well-ventilated area with at least 10 feet of clearance from walls and deck railings.
  • Never use a gas grill inside a home, garage, tent, or enclosed patio space.
  • Keep the grill away from combustible materials and overhangs.
  • Follow the manufacturer's instructions for lighting—always open the lid before igniting.
  • Clean grease trays and catch pans regularly to prevent flare-ups and grease fires.
  • Store propane cylinders outdoors according to local safety requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best gas grill under $500?

The Weber Spirit E-325 is a strong all-around candidate if its current price is within your budget. The Monument Mesa 410MBZ is worth comparing if you want additional features like an infrared sear zone, while the Nexgrill 4-burner is a good option to investigate for larger cooking capacity.

Is a three-burner grill enough for a family?

For many small and medium households, a three-burner grill provides plenty of cooking space (usually 400 to 450 sq. in.). The right choice depends on how many people you cook for and the types of meals you prepare.

Are four-burner gas grills better than three-burner grills?

Four-burner models generally offer more distinct cooking zones and provide greater cooking space. However, a well-designed three-burner grill can still be a better purchase if you prefer a smaller patio footprint or stronger brand support.

Should I buy a grill with a side burner?

A side burner is useful if you want to prepare sauces, warm side dishes, or saute onions separately without smoking up the indoor kitchen. If you do not expect to use it, you may prefer a simpler model with more side shelf prep space.

How long should a gas grill last?

Lifespan depends on construction quality, weather exposure, cleaning habits, frequency of use, and replacement-part availability. Regular maintenance, deep cleaning, and using a quality weather cover can easily extend the life of a sub-$500 grill to 5–10 years or more.

Final Verdict: Which Gas Grill Under $500 Should You Buy?

The best gas grill depends on how you plan to use it.

The Weber Spirit E-325 is our top all-around recommendation for everyday backyard grilling with dependable heat and dedicated searing. The Monument Mesa 410MBZ offers the most high-tech features for its price category, while the Nexgrill 4-Burner provides a massive cooking setup with a side burner for entertaining.

If keeping costs down is your primary priority, the Char-Broil Performance Series provides solid four-burner value. And for camping trips, tailgates, and small balcony spaces, the Weber Traveler is unmatched in folding portability.
