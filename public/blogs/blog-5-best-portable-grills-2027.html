How to Choose the Best Portable Grill in 2027

Before buying a portable grill, consider how and where you plan to use it. The right choice depends on your preferred fuel type, available vehicle cargo space, cooking capacity, and transportation needs.

1. Choose the Right Fuel Type

Propane Grills: Liquid propane models are the most popular portable grills because they heat up rapidly (ready in 5–10 minutes) and offer straightforward dial temperature control. They run on standard 1-lb green disposable cylinders or can be hooked up to 20-lb tanks using adapter hoses.

Charcoal Grills: Ideal for anyone who values authentic charcoal flavor and searing bark. They require more prep time (lighting coals) and careful ash cleanup, but require zero fuel tanks or regulators.

Electric Portable Grills: Suitable for condo balconies or glamping sites where open flame grills are restricted and AC outlets are accessible.

2. Consider Cooking Surface Area

Grill space determines how many portions you can cook concurrently:

  • Under 150 sq. in. (e.g. Smokey Joe): Great for 1–2 people or a fast solo trail cookout.
  • 150 to 200 sq. in. (e.g. Weber Q1200): Ideal for couples and small family meals (4–8 burgers).
  • 200 to 300 sq. in. (e.g. RoadTrip 285, Cuisinart CGG-306): Great for mid-size groups and simultaneous side dishes.
  • Above 300 sq. in. (e.g. Weber Traveler): Best for tailgating crowds and multi-course outdoor feasts.

3. Check Weight and Portability Style

Tabletop grills (like the Q1200 or Cuisinart) weigh between 20 and 30 lbs and slide easily into a trunk or camper compartment, but require a sturdy picnic table or tailgate. Scissor-cart grills (like the Traveler or RoadTrip) stand independently on their own legs and wheel around like luggage, but weigh 45–50 lbs.

4. Look for Temperature Control & Burner Count

Having multiple independent burners (such as the 3 burners on the RoadTrip 285 or 2 on the Cuisinart) lets you establish distinct heat zones: high heat for searing steaks, low heat for slow warming buns and sides.

5. Consider Cleaning and Maintenance

Look for accessible grease management—slide-out drip pans, removable grease trays, and porcelain-enameled or stainless-steel cooking grates that can be brushed and wiped down quickly at a campsite.

Safety Tips for Using Portable Grills

  • Always operate portable grills outdoors in open, well-ventilated areas. Never use gas or charcoal grills inside tents, RVs, vans, or enclosed garages.
  • Set up on a flat, stable, heat-resistant surface away from dry grass, low-hanging tree branches, and combustible canvas.
  • Check propane cylinder O-rings and hose connections for leaks with soapy water before igniting.
  • Never leave an active portable grill unattended, especially around children and pets.
  • Keep a small fire extinguisher or bucket of water/sand nearby at campsites.
  • Allow charcoal embers to cool for several hours—or douse thoroughly—before disposing in designated park ash cans.
  • Let the grill body cool down 100% before folding, latching the lid, or storing in your car trunk.
  • Adhere to regional seasonal fire bans and campsite regulations during dry summer periods.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best overall portable grill in 2027?

The Weber Traveler is our top pick for 2027. It pairs a massive 390 sq. in. cooking surface with a fluid, one-handed folding cart, making it the closest you can get to full-size backyard performance in a portable format.

Are portable grills worth buying?

Yes. They unlock outdoor cooking anywhere—from beach sunsets and tailgate stadiums to car camping trips and small apartment patios—without the high cost and permanence of a stationary grill.

Can I connect a portable grill to a standard 20-lb propane tank?

Yes. Most portable propane grills (including Weber, Coleman, and Cuisinart) accept an inexpensive propane adapter hose, allowing you to hook up directly to standard 20-lb barbecue propane tanks for extended backyard or basecamp sessions.

Can I use a portable grill on an apartment balcony?

It depends on your building's rules and municipal fire codes. Many residential codes prohibit charcoal grills and large gas cylinders on balconies, though small 1-lb propane tabletop grills or electric models are often permitted. Always verify local rules first.

How do you clean a portable grill at a campsite?

While grates are still warm, brush them with a brass or bristle-free grill scraper. Once cool, wipe down grease trays, empty the drip pan into a sealable container or trash, and wipe interior surfaces with biodegradable dish soap and warm water.
