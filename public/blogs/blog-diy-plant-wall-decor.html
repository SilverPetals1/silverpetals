DIY Plant Wall Decor Ideas for Small Spaces: Budget-Friendly Home Decor Inspiration

H
Written by SilverPetals • July 12, 2026 • 15 min read
Living in a small apartment or cozy flat doesn't mean you have to sacrifice the fresh, life-giving energy of houseplants. When floor space is limited, the smartest solution is to look up and utilize your empty wall space. Creating a DIY plant wall is a fantastic, budget-friendly way to introduce vertical greenery into your home without crowding your layout. By hanging trailing plants, mounting wooden shelves, and utilizing creative containers, you can transform cold drywall into a living, breathing work of art. The best part? You don't need a massive budget or advanced carpentry skills to build a stunning vertical display. In this guide, we review 15 functional DIY plant wall decor ideas to help you bring natural beauty indoors on a budget.

1. Wall-Mounted Macrame Plant Hangers

Wall-mounted macrame plant hangers are classic bohemian accessories that hold potted plants securely on walls. Made of woven cotton cord, these hangers add beautiful texture and natural warmth. Simply hang them from a wall hook or nail, and insert a small potted plant like a pothos or spider plant. This is a very budget-friendly project, costing only $10 to $15 for materials.

2. DIY Pegboard System for Adjustable Plant Shelves

A wooden wall pegboard is a highly functional storage solution that lets you rearrange plant shelves easily. Mount a pegboard to your wall, and insert wooden dowels and shelves to support small potted plants, cuttings, and toys. This adjustable system keeps plants organized and allows you to adjust spacing as your plants grow. A basic pegboard setup costs around $25 to $35 for materials.

3. Repurposed Wooden Crates as Vertical Planters

Repurposing old wooden shipping crates is a rustic, budget-friendly way to build deep vertical wall storage. Mount two or three wooden crates to your wall, and arrange potted plants inside them. The flat crate tops also serve as extra shelves for trailing leaves. This rustic planter layout costs around $15 to $20 if you upcycle old crates.

4. Command Hook Hanging Planters for Renter-Friendly Setups

For rental apartments where drilling is not allowed, adhesive Command hooks are great renter-friendly tools. Stick the hooks onto a clean wall, and suspend lightweight plastic or woven pots using twine. This is an affordable, damage-free styling solution that allows you to add greenery to narrow hallways. The total project cost is around $12 to $18.

5. A Vertical Pallet Planter for Outdoor-Inspired Walls

A vertical wood pallet planter is a rustic, eco-friendly way to grow herbs and small plants indoors. Secure backing fabric to the underside of a wooden pallet, fill it with potting soil, and insert small plants between the wood slats. Lean the pallet against a wall or mount it securely. This is a very cheap DIY project, costing under $15 if you source a free shipping pallet.

6. Floating Shelves Styled with a Mix of Trailing Plants

Floating shelves are classic design choices that display plants beautifully without using floor space. Mount two or three slim shelves on a wall, and style them with trailing pothos or heartleaf philodendron. The cascading vines drape down the wall, creating a lush green curtain. A simple floating shelf setup costs around $20 to $30.

7. DIY Wall-Mounted Terrarium Display

Wall-mounted glass terrariums are stylish ways to showcase small succulents and air plants. Mount these half-globe glass containers directly to your wall, and fill them with pebbles, soil, and slow-growing plants. The clear glass lets daylight highlight the plant shapes, creating an artistic display. A three-piece terrarium set costs around $25 to $35.

8. Test Tube Wall Planters for a Modern, Minimal Look

Glass test tube propagation vases are clean, modern choices for growing plant cuttings in water. Mount a wooden holder containing three or four test tubes to your wall, and insert water and fresh plant cuttings. This is a great way to grow new houseplants on a budget, costing around $10 to $15 for materials.

9. Repurposed Picture Frames as Living Plant Art

Turn an old wooden picture frame into a living plant display by mounting small air plants or moss inside the frame outline. Use thin wire mesh to hold moss in place, and insert low-water succulents. Hang the frame on your wall to create a custom living frame centerpiece. Upcycling old picture frames costs around $10 to $18 for materials.

10. A Ladder Shelf Styled with Cascading Plants

A wooden ladder shelf is a great, space-saving display option that fits tightly in empty corners. Arrange potted plants on the shelves, placing trailing plants on the top shelf so they can cascade down the sides. The natural wood design adds warmth and style, while the vertical layout keeps plants organized. A basic ladder shelf costs around $30 to $45.

11. DIY Felt or Fabric Pocket Wall Planters

Felt pocket planters are lightweight, breathable vertical gardens that mount easily to walls. The breathable felt pockets allow roots to grow healthily and make watering simple. Hang a multi-pocket planter on an entryway wall and fill it with herbs or small ferns. A felt wall planter costs around $15 to $22.

12. Magnetic Plant Holders for Metal Surfaces

Magnetic pocket planters are fun, space-saving choices for metal refrigerators or metal door frames. These tiny pocket containers cling securely to metal surfaces, holding small air plants or succulents without drilling holes. They add easy greenery to kitchen spaces. A set of three magnetic planters costs around $10 to $15.

13. A Window-Adjacent Vertical Garden for Natural Light

Hanging a vertical plant shelf near a bright window ensures your plants get plenty of natural daylight. Use thin wire cords to hang wooden shelves directly in front of a window, and arrange your plants on them. This vertical layout keeps plants healthy and creates a beautiful green window screen. The materials cost around $20 to $30.

14. Upcycled Tin Can Wall Planters for a Budget-Friendly Look

Upcycling clean, empty tin cans is an affordable, eco-friendly way to make small wall planters. Paint the metal cans in coordinating colors, drill drainage holes in the bottom, and mount them to a wooden board or directly to your wall. This project costs under $10, making it a very cheap DIY choice.

15. A Curated Mix of Faux and Real Plants for Low-Maintenance Greenery

Mixing real and high-quality faux plants is a great, low-maintenance choice for dark rooms or high, hard-to-reach shelves. Place real, low-water plants like pothos on lower shelves, and use faux ivy on higher shelves. This blends real textures with maintenance-free greenery, keeping your display green year-round. The total cost is around $25 to $40.
